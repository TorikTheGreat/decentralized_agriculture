\section{The proposed solution}

\subsection{Concept overview}

The platform is a small-scale, open-source, sensor-driven greenhouse with the following operational loop:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A sensor array takes periodic measurements of all relevant environmental variables (temperature, relative humidity, CO\textsubscript{2} concentration, pH, electrical conductivity, and light intensity).
  \item A camera periodically images the crop. A computer vision model extracts growth metrics, identifies disease symptoms, and can identify species.
  \item An ML growth model --- trained on the shared dataset and running inference at the edge --- computes optimal environmental set-points to maximise yield while minimising resource consumption for the current species and growth stage.
  \item A PID controller on a microcontroller drives the actuators (LED grow lights, circulation fans, nutrient dosing pumps, heating/cooling elements) to reach and maintain the computed set-points.
  \item Anonymised telemetry is periodically uploaded to a shared data commons, where it is used to retrain models, which are then pushed back to all nodes via over-the-air updates.
  \item A climate-aware species recommendation engine suggests crops whose optimal conditions best match the local ambient environment.
\end{enumerate}

All hardware, firmware, software, models, and data are open source.

The platform introduces one new risk not present in field agriculture: \textbf{energy intensity}. Controlled environment agriculture trades land and water efficiency for electrical energy consumption. Whether this trade is environmentally beneficial depends on the local energy mix. This is addressed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Scope and niche}

The platform is not designed to replace all agriculture. Commodity grain production (wheat, rice, maize) remains outside the scope of small-scale CEA, and field agriculture will continue to supply these staples. The platform's objective is \textbf{nutritional self-sufficiency}: the ability to produce, in proximity to consumers, the full spectrum of macronutrients and micronutrients needed for health, using a diversified crop portfolio that extends well beyond the leafy greens and tomatoes of conventional commercial CEA.

This portfolio includes: calorie-dense tubers (potatoes, sweet potatoes) that can also be processed into storable flours and starches; mushrooms, which are already a mainstream CEA crop, require no light, and provide protein on waste substrates; oilseed legumes (peanuts, soybeans) whose caloric density exceeds that of wheat flour and which yield storable oils and protein-rich flours; and micronutrient-dense vegetables, herbs, and fruits for which the transport, water, and pesticide savings of CEA are largest. Some of these crops are well-established in CEA (mushrooms, leafy greens, tubers); others (oilseed legumes, seed crops) are plausible but largely unproven and constitute R\&D targets for the project. The species recommendation engine selects crops that optimise simultaneously for nutritional completeness, caloric contribution, and energy efficiency --- matching the crop portfolio to the local climate to minimise the energy cost of environmental control.

This framing positions the platform as a complement to, not a replacement for, field agriculture: it supplies the diverse produce, legumes, and tubers that healthier dietary patterns require (see Section~\ref{sec:nutrition}), while commodity grains continue to be produced in fields --- at lower volume if the dietary shift away from animal feed crops documented in the problem analysis is realised.

The economic case for the platform is not yet fully developed and will be a primary output of the R\&D phase. The critical question is whether ML-optimised, open-source CEA can reach cost parity with conventional produce for the target crop portfolio --- and under what conditions (climate zone, energy source, scale) it does so. This analysis is deferred to a dedicated economic assessment once energy consumption and yield data from prototype deployments are available.
